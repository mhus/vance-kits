# Debt Types and Shapes — debt isn't one thing

"Debt" lumps many different kinds of obligation that
behave very differently. Treating them as one category
loses the structure that determines what to do.

This manual maps the common types and their relevant
properties.

## Properties that matter

For any debt, what to know:

- **Interest rate.** Cost of carrying.
- **Fixed or variable?** Variable can rise.
- **Term.** How long until paid off (in scheduled
  payments).
- **Secured or unsecured?** What can the lender take if
  you don't pay?
- **Tax treatment.** Deductible? Pre-tax pay back?
  Forgiveness taxable?
- **Dischargeability in bankruptcy.** Some debts survive
  bankruptcy in many jurisdictions.
- **Effect on credit.** How much it hurts a missed
  payment.
- **Refinance possibility.** Can you swap to a better
  rate later?
- **Prepayment penalty.** Cost to pay early.

These properties differ wildly across debt types. The
"highest interest rate" rule treats them as fungible;
they aren't.

## Common debt types

### Credit card debt

- **Interest:** very high (often 18-30% APR in the US;
  higher elsewhere).
- **Fixed/variable:** typically variable.
- **Term:** revolving (no fixed end date if minimum
  paid).
- **Secured:** unsecured.
- **Tax:** generally non-deductible.
- **Bankruptcy:** generally dischargeable.
- **Credit:** late payments hurt; high utilisation
  hurts.
- **Refinance:** to a lower-rate personal loan or
  balance-transfer card; available depending on
  credit.

Generally the worst debt to carry. Compounding +
high rate + minimum-payment-extends-forever pattern.

### Mortgage

- **Interest:** historically lower than most other
  consumer debt (varies hugely by country and time).
- **Fixed/variable:** usually fixed (US, parts of EU);
  often variable (UK, Canada, etc.).
- **Term:** typically 15-30 years.
- **Secured:** by the property.
- **Tax:** sometimes deductible (jurisdiction-
  specific; rules change).
- **Bankruptcy:** complex; varies; often the home
  is at stake.
- **Credit:** late payments hurt severely.
- **Refinance:** common, depends on rates and credit.
- **Prepayment penalty:** sometimes (read your
  contract).

### Student loans

- **Interest:** varies widely — government loans
  often subsidised; private loans market-rate.
- **Fixed/variable:** depends on origin.
- **Term:** typically 10-25 years; income-driven
  plans extend.
- **Secured:** unsecured.
- **Tax:** sometimes interest-deductible (very
  jurisdiction-specific).
- **Bankruptcy:** in many countries (especially US),
  notoriously difficult to discharge.
- **Forgiveness programmes:** US has PSLF, IDR
  forgiveness; other countries have similar but
  different. Important to understand your specific
  programme rules.
- **Refinance:** possible; can lose government-
  programme eligibility if refinanced to private.

### Auto loan

- **Interest:** moderate (varies with credit).
- **Fixed/variable:** typically fixed.
- **Term:** 36-84 months (longer terms now common).
- **Secured:** by the vehicle.
- **Tax:** generally non-deductible.
- **Bankruptcy:** complex; vehicle is at stake.
- **Credit:** standard impact.
- **Refinance:** possible, less common than mortgage
  refi.

A common trap: long terms (72-84 months) keep monthly
payments low but accumulate interest and lock in
underwater positions (owing more than the car is
worth).

### Personal loan

- **Interest:** mid-range (varies hugely).
- **Fixed/variable:** typically fixed.
- **Term:** 2-7 years usually.
- **Secured:** can be either.
- **Tax:** generally non-deductible (consumer use).
- **Bankruptcy:** generally dischargeable.
- **Refinance:** sometimes used to consolidate
  higher-interest debt (e.g. credit card → personal
  loan).

Consolidation loans can be useful (lower rate, fixed
schedule, pays down rather than carries). Or can be a
trap if it just restructures without addressing
underlying spending.

### Payday loan / very high-interest short-term

- **Interest:** extremely high (300-700%+ effective
  APR).
- **Term:** weeks.
- **Secured:** sometimes by next-paycheck.
- **Tax:** non-deductible.
- **Bankruptcy:** typically dischargeable but the
  damage is already done.

**These are predatory.** If user is taking payday
loans, this is a crisis signal — direct to non-profit
credit counselling immediately.

### Buy-now-pay-later (BNPL)

- **Interest:** variable; some 0% if paid on time;
  high penalties if late.
- **Term:** weeks to months.
- **Secured:** generally unsecured.
- **Tax:** non-deductible.
- **Reporting:** sometimes to credit bureaus, sometimes
  not (varies).

The "0% if paid on time" framing hides that late or
missed payments trigger high charges. Easy to
accumulate across multiple BNPL providers.

### Medical debt

- **Interest:** often 0% if not in collections;
  high if collections.
- **Term:** depends on negotiation with provider.
- **Secured:** generally unsecured.
- **Tax:** medical expenses sometimes deductible
  (varies).
- **Bankruptcy:** often dischargeable.
- **Negotiable:** medical bills are notoriously
  negotiable (especially before going to
  collections).

If facing medical debt, negotiation often more
productive than just paying.

### Tax debt

- **Interest:** moderate to high (tax authority
  rates).
- **Penalties:** can compound severely.
- **Secured:** government can lien; especially in US,
  IRS has very strong collection powers.
- **Bankruptcy:** complex; some tax debt
  dischargeable, some not.
- **Negotiable:** offer-in-compromise, payment plans,
  hardship programmes exist.

**Get a tax professional early** for substantial tax
debt. Penalties can be reduced; payment plans can be
negotiated.

### Family / friend debt

- **Interest:** typically none.
- **Term:** open / informal.
- **Secured:** rarely formally.
- **Tax:** depends; gifts vs. loans treatment.
- **Bankruptcy:** legally dischargeable; relationally
  catastrophic.

The relational cost is the dominant factor. Treat
family debt as a relationship issue more than a
financial one.

### Business debt (personally guaranteed)

- **Interest:** varies.
- **Secured:** often by personal assets if
  personally guaranteed.
- **Bankruptcy:** can flow through to personal
  bankruptcy.

If you're a small business owner with personally
guaranteed debt, the business risk and personal risk
are entangled. Worth professional input on
structure.

## Variations by jurisdiction

These properties vary substantially by country and
sometimes by state/province:

- Bankruptcy treatment of student loans, tax debt,
  mortgages.
- Statute of limitations on debt collection.
- Tax deductibility of interest.
- Foreclosure / repossession process.
- Credit reporting rules.
- Debt collection regulation.

Don't assume what's true in country X applies in your
country Y. For substantial decisions, jurisdiction-
specific input is essential.

## Mapping your debt picture

Before deciding what to do, list your debts with:

| Debt | Balance | Rate | Term | Tax-deductible? | Secured by? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Credit card 1 | $5,200 | 24% APR | revolving | No | unsecured |
| Mortgage | $290,000 | 4.2% fixed | 22 years remaining | maybe (jurisdiction) | home |
| Student loan | $18,500 | 5.5% fixed | 7 years remaining | partially | unsecured |
| Auto loan | $14,000 | 6.0% fixed | 4 years remaining | car |

The picture together is what matters. With this
picture, prioritisation (`prioritising-debts`) and
"don't pay off early" decisions (`when-not-to-pay-off
-early`) become tractable.

## Anti-patterns

### "Debt is debt"

Treating all debt as the same. Different shapes warrant
different approaches.

### Tunnel vision on rate

The rate matters but isn't the only dimension. A
high-rate small balance hurts less than a low-rate
large balance over time. A secured debt has different
risk-of-default than unsecured.

### Ignoring tax effects

Interest deductibility (where applicable) shifts the
effective rate. Tax-deductible 6% mortgage interest
might cost ~4-4.5% after deduction; non-deductible 6%
auto loan costs 6%.

But: tax effects vary by jurisdiction and individual.
Don't assume; verify with tax professional.

### Forgetting refinance optionality

Some debt is refinanceable; some isn't. Decisions
about which to pay off first can hinge on which can
be refinanced to lower rates later.

### Treating family debt as priority

Mathematically, 0% family debt should be paid last
(after market-rate debts). Relationally, it can be
weighing on the relationship; sometimes paying it off
first is the right move.

This is a personal call; the math and the relationship
can pull in different directions.
