# When to Stop and Call Counsel — bright-line rules

Some situations warrant attorney now, not later. This
manual is about recognising those situations and not
attempting layperson navigation.

The general principle: when in doubt, attorney. This
manual is about the cases where the doubt should be
resolved emphatically toward attorney.

## Bright-line: stop now

For any of these, stop the layperson conversation
immediately and direct to attorney:

### Active legal proceedings

- You've been served with a complaint / summons.
- You've received a subpoena.
- You're a party in active litigation.
- A court order has been issued affecting you.
- You're in arbitration.
- You've been notified of a regulatory investigation
  / enforcement action.

For these:

- Attorney *now*. Same day if possible.
- Many proceedings have very short response windows
  (days, sometimes).
- Missing windows can forfeit rights.
- DIY responses can damage the case.

### Criminal matters (any side)

- You're being investigated.
- You're a suspect.
- You're a witness in a criminal matter.
- You're considering reporting a crime.
- You're a victim seeking restitution.
- You're charged with anything.
- A search warrant has been mentioned / executed.
- Police have asked to speak with you.

For criminal matters:

- Attorney *now*. Don't speak with police without
  attorney (in most jurisdictions, you have the
  right not to).
- "I have nothing to hide" doesn't mean you should
  speak without counsel.
- Even as a witness, attorney consult is sometimes
  warranted.
- For victims: attorney can help navigate the
  process and rights.

**No exceptions.** Even minor charges. Even "I'll
plead guilty anyway". Even "I just want to get this
over with."

### Imminent deadlines

- Court filing deadline within days.
- Statute of limitations approaching.
- Contract response deadline (cure period;
  acceptance window).
- Arbitration / mediation response.
- Tax deadlines with potential legal implications.

For any time-pressured legal matter:

- Attorney as early as possible.
- The "I have time" assumption is often wrong.
- Lost-deadline consequences are often
  non-recoverable.

### Threats and counter-threats

- You've been threatened with legal action.
- You're considering threatening someone with legal
  action.
- A "cease and desist" letter has been received.
- A "demand letter" has been received.

For these:

- Attorney before responding.
- Improper response can damage your position.
- Not responding can also damage.
- Attorney advises on whether / how to respond.

### Accidents / injuries

- You've been involved in an accident.
- Someone has been injured (you or others) due to
  alleged fault.
- You've received a claim from an insurance company.
- An insurance adjuster has called.

For these:

- Don't make statements without attorney.
- Don't sign documents without attorney review.
- Insurance companies often try to settle for less
  than full value.

### Family matters with active issues

- Divorce or separation proceedings.
- Custody disputes.
- Domestic violence / restraining order.
- Child welfare investigations.
- Estate / inheritance disputes.
- Adoption proceedings.

Family law moves fast and has substantial long-term
consequences.

### Real estate / substantial transactions

- Closing on a property in days.
- Substantial business transaction.
- Settlement of any kind.
- Significant inheritance / estate matter.

These shouldn't proceed without attorney.

### Substantial financial stakes

- Tax issues with substantial amounts.
- Bankruptcy considerations.
- Foreclosure / repossession threatened.
- Substantial business losses with potential
  liability.
- Investments / partnerships with large amounts.

The cost of attorney at small fraction of stakes;
not consulting is usually a mistake.

### Employment terminations / discrimination

- You're being terminated and considering whether to
  contest.
- You believe you've experienced discrimination /
  harassment / retaliation.
- You've been asked to sign a severance / release
  agreement.
- You're under formal investigation by employer.

Employment matters often have time limits; severance
agreements often have signing deadlines that come
fast.

### Anything involving liberty

- Immigration matters with deportation potential.
- Child custody with placement risk.
- Contempt of court risk.
- Probation / parole matters.

### When you don't understand the situation

If you can't articulate what's happening clearly: that's
itself a signal. Get someone who can — that's an
attorney.

## Common reasons people delay attorney

And why they're often wrong:

### "It's not that serious"

Often is. Legal matters often have implications
beyond what's apparent. Triage for seriousness is
itself attorney territory.

### "I can't afford it"

Free / low-cost options exist:

- Public defender (criminal).
- Legal aid (civil; means-tested).
- Bar referral (low-cost consult).
- Pro bono programmes.
- Free initial consultations.
- Law school clinics.
- Some attorneys take on contingency (you pay only
  if win).

Cost is rarely a complete bar to consultation.

### "I don't want to make it bigger"

Getting attorney input doesn't escalate. The other
side often has attorney already; you having one
levels the field.

### "I'll handle it; if it doesn't work, I'll get
attorney"

Many legal matters have one-shot dynamics. Mishandling
the early stages can foreclose options. The mistake
isn't always recoverable.

### "I'll wait until I hear back"

Some deadlines pass while you wait. Filing windows;
response windows; statute of limitations.

### "It's too complicated; I'm not sure what to ask"

Attorneys are used to "I don't know what I should
ask". The intake is part of their service.

### "I trust the other party"

Sometimes warranted; sometimes not. Trust + attorney
review = both. Trust + no review = exposure.

### "I've handled similar before"

Each matter is its own. "Similar" can hide critical
differences.

### "It's just a formality"

Sometimes. Other times "just a formality" is the
moment when legal commitments are made.

## When attorney is essential vs. when it's
recommended

Some matters — bright-line stop-now. Above.

Others — strong recommendation but layperson handling
sometimes possible:

- Simple contracts in routine matters.
- Initial review of documents that haven't yet been
  signed.
- General orientation on a topic.

The line shifts based on stakes:

- $500 dispute over a service: layperson can handle
  through small claims if needed.
- $50,000 dispute: attorney essential.
- $500,000 dispute: attorney + perhaps multiple
  specialists.

## How to find an attorney

For users new to it:

### Bar association referrals

Your jurisdiction's bar association typically has a
referral service. Often offers low-cost initial
consult.

### Legal aid (civil)

For lower-income individuals. Means-tested. Often
focused on family, housing, employment, public
benefits.

### Public defender (criminal)

Constitutional right in US (and similar in other
jurisdictions). Don't skip.

### Specialised lawyer-locator services

Some areas have specialised matchmaking — e.g.
employment law, immigration, tenant rights.

### Word of mouth

Sometimes useful; verify the attorney's actual
specialty (general practitioners may not have
expertise for your specific matter).

### Online directories

Avvo, Martindale-Hubbell, FindLaw, similar. Quality
varies; cross-check.

### Bar associations (specialty)

Many bars have specialty sections (criminal defence,
family law, business). Their referral services
match by speciality.

## What to do during the wait

If attorney consult will take time:

- **Don't make statements that could harm position.**
- **Don't sign documents.**
- **Document carefully** (see
  `dispute-framing/documentation-discipline`).
- **Preserve evidence** — emails, texts, photos.
- **Don't discuss the matter** beyond people who need
  to know (spouse, attorney once retained).
- **For criminal matters: don't speak with law
  enforcement** without attorney.

## Anti-patterns

### "I'll figure it out"

Can be true for ordinary matters; rarely for
substantial legal exposure.

### Trying to be your own attorney

Self-representation in litigation ("pro se") is
allowed but disadvantaged. For non-trivial matters,
substantial disadvantage.

### Talking to the other side without attorney

Statements to opposing counsel / opposing party can
be used against you. Even informal conversations can
have legal implications.

### Trying to negotiate without understanding what's
on the table

Without legal understanding, you don't know what
you're negotiating away. The "settlement" might
involve releases / waivers you don't recognise.

### Waiting for things to escalate

Things often escalate. Position weakens with delay.
Earlier consult is generally cheaper than later.

### Substituting AI / online research for attorney

Legal AI / online resources can help you prepare for
attorney consult; can't substitute for it.
