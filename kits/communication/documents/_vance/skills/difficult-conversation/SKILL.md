The user is facing a conversation they're dreading. Some
combination of high stakes, an uncomfortable message, and
emotional weight on either side. The work is two-part:
**preparing** before the conversation, and **navigating**
during it.

Preparation isn't memorising a speech. It's clarifying
what you actually want to communicate, what the other
person needs from this exchange, and what you'll do if
their response is X / Y / Z.

## Default protocol

1. **Why this conversation, why now?** Surface the
   driving reason. If the user can't articulate it, they
   may not be ready — preparation is premature.
2. **What's the message?** One or two sentences. The
   actual thing you want them to hear. If it takes a
   paragraph, you haven't pinned it.
3. **What does the other person likely hear?** Not what
   you intend; what lands. Often diverges from what you
   say.
4. **Plan, but don't script.** Outlines work; word-for-
   word scripts collapse on contact. See
   `preparation-script`.
5. **Manage the emotional thermometer.** Yours and
   theirs. The conversation fails when temperature runs
   too hot for either side. See `emotional-thermometer`.
6. **Land the message; then listen.** Most users
   under-listen after delivering. Holding silence is
   harder than it looks. See `landing-and-listening`.

## On-demand manuals

- `preparation-script` — what to plan vs. what to leave
  open. The "core sentence" technique. Anticipating
  responses without rehearsing each. Naming the elephant
  in the room. When the script is the problem.
- `emotional-thermometer` — recognising heat in yourself
  and the other person. Cooling moves; pause moves. When
  to break and resume. What to do when one side flares
  past productive range.
- `landing-and-listening` — after the message is
  delivered. Sitting with silence. Listening for what
  the other person actually says vs. what you expected.
  Distinguishing reaction from response.

## Hard rules

- **Don't deliver bad news in writing if a face-to-face
  is feasible.** Email-firing / text-breakup signals
  cowardice and removes the listening half of the
  conversation.
- **Don't ambush.** "Can we talk for a minute" without
  warning loads anxiety on the other person. Give a
  minimum honest preview ("I want to talk about
  ⟨topic⟩").
- **Don't soften so much the message is lost.** "I think
  there might be a small concern that's possibly
  worth..." — by the time you finish, they don't know
  there's a concern. Direct beats hedge.
- **Don't go in if you're flooded.** If you're so angry
  / hurt / scared that you can't think, postpone.
  Decisions made under flooding are worse than
  decisions made calm-and-late.
- **Don't moralise.** "You should be ashamed" / "How
  could you" — closes the conversation. Even when
  you're right. Surface the fact, not the moral.
- **Don't rehearse to win.** Preparation is for
  clarity, not for victory-by-superior-rhetoric. If you
  enter the conversation with rebuttals queued, you
  haven't planned a conversation; you've planned a
  monologue.
- **Acknowledge professional help when relevant.** Some
  difficult conversations (abuse, mental-health crises,
  unsafe situations) warrant a therapist / mediator /
  HR / authorities, not just preparation. Surface the
  option; don't push past it.
