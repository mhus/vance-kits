The user wants to read for *learning* (not for
entertainment) and retain what they read. The default
mode of reading — start at page 1, read through to the
end — is often the wrong mode for learning purposes.

Different reading goals warrant different techniques.
Skim-reading a chapter has its place; deep-reading a
paragraph has its place. Knowing which to apply when is
the skill.

## Default protocol

1. **Why are you reading this?** Skim for orientation;
   deep-read for understanding; reference-read for
   specific information; entertainment-read for the
   experience. Goal drives method.
2. **Match technique to goal.** See `format-by-goal`.
3. **Pre-read first.** Especially for non-fiction — read
   table of contents, intro, conclusion, headings
   before diving in. Builds the scaffold for the
   detail to land in.
4. **Active engagement during reading.** Notes,
   marginalia, periodic recall pauses. See
   `reading-for-retention`.
5. **Multi-pass for important material.** First pass:
   skim for shape. Second pass: deep on what matters.
   See `second-pass-techniques`.
6. **Don't finish for completion's sake.** If a book /
   paper isn't serving the goal, stop. Reading every
   page doesn't equal value.

## On-demand manuals

- `reading-for-retention` — active reading techniques.
  Marginalia, summary-after-each-section, periodic
  pause-and-recall. The discipline that converts
  passive reading into retainable learning.
- `second-pass-techniques` — multi-pass reading. Skim
  first, then deep on what merits it. When second
  pass is worth doing; when one pass suffices.
- `format-by-goal` — different formats (book / paper /
  article / textbook) warrant different reading
  approaches. The "how to read a paper" question; how
  to read a textbook chapter; how to read a long-form
  essay. Mismatched approach is a common waste.

If you're starting a new piece, walk `format-by-goal`
first to set up the right approach.

## Hard rules

- **Reading isn't learning.** Reading is input. Without
  recall / application / writing, the material doesn't
  consolidate. Plan the post-read step.
- **Don't read cover-to-cover by default.** Many
  books / papers don't earn cover-to-cover. Read
  selectively unless the material genuinely warrants
  full attention.
- **Don't speed-read deep material.** Speed-reading
  works for shallow material; for material requiring
  understanding, the rate-limit is comprehension, not
  eye speed.
- **Don't highlight-as-engagement.** Highlighting feels
  active; produces minimal retention. Use highlights
  as candidate-extraction (turn into cards / notes
  later), not as the engagement itself.
- **Don't keep reading past saturation.** Cognitive
  capacity is limited per session. When you stop
  retaining, stop reading.
- **Don't assume you'll remember.** "I'll remember
  this" is the lie that produces lost learning. Take
  the note now.
- **Don't give up too easily on hard material.**
  Conversely: the hardest, most-rewarding learning
  often comes from struggle. Stop because of
  diminishing returns, not because of difficulty.
