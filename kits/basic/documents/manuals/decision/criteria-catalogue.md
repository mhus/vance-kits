# Decision Criteria — Catalogue

A reference list of criterion buckets you can offer when the user is
stuck articulating what they actually care about. Not a checklist —
pick the three to five that fit the decision.

## Resource criteria

- **Money cost** — upfront, ongoing, opportunity cost.
- **Time cost** — calendar time, focused work hours, learning curve.
- **Attention cost** — how many running threads does this add?
- **Energy cost** — does this drain or refill the user?

## Reversibility criteria

- **Two-way vs. one-way door** — cheap to undo, or committing?
- **Exit cost** — if it doesn't work, what does it take to back out?
- **Lock-in risk** — does this constrain future decisions?
- **Sunk-cost trap** — will I keep going because I already invested?

## Impact criteria

- **Self-impact** — health, time, finances, growth.
- **Close-circle impact** — partner, family, close friends.
- **Team / professional impact** — colleagues, reports, customers.
- **Public impact** — visibility, reputation, legal exposure.

## Learning criteria

- **Skill acquisition** — will this teach me something I want to
  know?
- **Information value** — will this resolve uncertainty I care
  about? (decisions that *teach* are sometimes worth taking even if
  the immediate ROI is negative)
- **Network value** — does this put me near interesting people?

## Fit criteria

- **Stated goals** — is this on the path I said I want?
- **Values fit** — does this align with what I claim to care about?
- **Energy fit** — does this match my season of life right now?
- **Identity fit** — does the future-me-after-this look like
  someone I'd want to be?

## Risk criteria

- **Risk of regret** — which option do I regret more if it goes
  wrong? (asymmetric)
- **Tail risk** — what's the worst plausible outcome?
- **Dependency risk** — am I betting on someone else's promise?
- **Reversibility-of-regret** — if this goes wrong, can I learn
  from it, or is it a permanent loss?

## Anti-criteria — watch for these leaking in

- **"Should" criteria** — ones that come from a borrowed
  imagination ("I should want X"). Probe: whose voice is this?
- **Precedent-driven** — "everyone else does it this way".
  Sometimes valid (defaults are signals); usually lazy.
- **FOMO-driven** — "if I don't take this now, I'll never get
  another chance". Often false. Check: how many similar chances
  did I see in the last year?
- **Loss-aversion-driven** — anchored to avoiding a loss rather
  than getting a gain. Reframe to neutral and re-evaluate.

## Operational tips

- Three to five criteria is the sweet spot. More than seven and
  the user is hiding behind a matrix.
- Weight the criteria *before* scoring. If all five feel equal,
  they're not — push for ranking.
- Some criteria have a hard floor: "must not violate X". Surface
  those first; options that fail the floor drop out before scoring.
