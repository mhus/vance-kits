# Article Review-and-Publish Process

This is how our content team takes an article from idea to
published. It is a **repeatable process**: the same steps run for
every article, so it is worth capturing as a reusable workflow
rather than improvised each time.

## The steps

1. **Draft.** A writer produces a full first draft from the topic
   brief. The draft must be a complete piece, not an outline.
2. **Editorial review (approval gate).** An editor reads the draft
   and makes ONE of two decisions:
   - **approve** — the draft is publishable as-is;
   - **request changes** — the draft goes back to the writer with
     concrete notes.
   This is a genuine human decision point: the process pauses here
   until the editor responds.
3. **Revise loop.** On *request changes*, the writer revises using
   the editor's notes and the draft returns to editorial review.
   This loop repeats — but it is **bounded**: after at most **three**
   revision rounds the piece is parked for a senior editor instead
   of looping forever.
4. **Publish.** On *approve*, the article is published and the
   process ends successfully.

## Why the gate matters

Publishing without the editorial approval step is the failure mode
this process exists to prevent. The approval gate is mandatory and
must be a real pause-for-a-human decision, not an automated check.

## Why the loop is bounded

An unbounded revise loop is how articles get stuck in limbo. The
three-round cap forces an escalation (senior editor) instead of an
infinite writer↔editor ping-pong.
